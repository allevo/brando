# Phase 15.5 — A full city turns people away, and the player can see it

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

**Goal:** once a city is past its founding trickle, immigration draws on the residents it already has,
so a bigger, equally attractive city grows faster than a smaller one; and every migrant the city could
not house is counted where the player can see it, not silently dropped.
**Depends on:** 15.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

Phase 15 rated immigration **per free place**, not per resident, to dodge a bootstrap trap: a
`hard`-profile city is founded with every house empty, and a rate proportional to the *population* is
`0 × anything = 0` forever, so such a city could never draw its first immigrant. That was written down
in phase 15's own "How it went" as a corrected-in-place design choice, not a settled one.

Rating immigration on the population it already has is the more intuitive model — a bigger, more
attractive city should draw people faster than a small one at the same attractiveness — and it is also
what phase 15's own original sketch asked for before the bootstrap problem forced the substitution.
Free places become a **cap** on how many of the people who want to move in actually can, rather than the
thing the rate is counted against.

And there is a second, equally important reason: today, a matured immigration event that finds no free
house is silently dropped inside `migration::immigration`'s placement loop, and nothing records that it
happened. The player has no way to tell "my city is attractive but out of room" from "my city just isn't
attractive" — and that distinction is exactly the signal that tells a player to build more houses.

The bootstrap trap has to be re-solved, not merely reintroduced. A tick-based window (immigration
behaves differently for the first N ticks of the game) was considered and rejected: it is tied to the
calendar regardless of what the player has actually built, so a slow start — or simply taking a while to
lay the first house on `hard` — could burn through a fixed number of ticks before a single resident ever
exists to receive one. A **population threshold** is tied to the city's own state instead, so it behaves
the same whether the player is fast or slow, and it hands off cleanly the moment the city has enough
residents for a word-of-mouth model to make sense.

## What gets built

### Two regimes, gated by population

```rust
if population < founding_population_threshold {
    // exactly phase 15's shipped formula, scoped to the founding window
    rate = free_places * founding_immigration_per_thousand_per_month * attractiveness;
} else {
    // the model this phase is for
    rate = population * immigration_per_thousand_per_month * attractiveness;
}
```

Below the threshold, immigration behaves exactly as phase 15 shipped it — proven to get a `hard` city
(born with nobody in any house) to a living population within five years, because the founding rate is
counted against free places, which exist from the moment a house is built regardless of who lives in it.
At or above the threshold, the rate switches to the population-proportional model: "my city is bigger
and more attractive, so it grows faster" becomes a real, felt property instead of an accident of free
places alone.

Both regimes keep `attractiveness` as the same multiplicative quality signal; nothing about
`migration::attractiveness` itself changes.

**The hand-off is a hard cutover, not a smooth blend.** The same kind of threshold the migration and
demographics tables already use elsewhere (`emigration_threshold`, `birth_threshold`) is a cliff, not a
curve, and that is what keeps the mechanic explainable and testable instead of adding a second kind of
curve to reason about. If the cutover produces a visible kink in the growth curve once the two rate
constants are chosen, that is balancing to watch in the dump, not a reason to add a blend.

### Two new fields in `migration`

In `sim-core/src/data.rs`'s `MigrationRules`, `sim-data`'s raw/validate, and `rules.ron`:

- `founding_immigration_per_thousand_per_month` — the founding-regime rate, counted per free place, the
  same unit `immigration_per_thousand_per_month` used before this phase.
- `founding_population_threshold` — the population at and above which the growth regime takes over.

`immigration_per_thousand_per_month` keeps its name and becomes genuinely "per thousand residents"
again, matching what phase 15's original sketch called it before the bootstrap problem forced the
free-places substitution.

**Validation.** `MigrationRules::is_off()` widens to require the founding rate at zero too, alongside
the existing two. The "no lone zero rate" shape check in `sim-data`'s `validate_migration` widens the
same way. `founding_population_threshold` gets the same `TooSmall { min: 1 }` field check the other
rates get when migration is not off. No new cross-table (`DataSet::inconsistencies`) relation looks
necessary — the threshold does not need to stand in a checked relation with any other table's number,
only with itself being positive.

### The player-facing signal: `turned_away`

A **diagnostic** counter, the same shape as `born`/`died`/`immigrated`/`emigrated` but explicitly **not**
part of `PopulationTotals::balance()`'s conservation equation — someone turned away never became a
resident, so counting them there would break the equality rather than complete it. The same distinction
`FoodTotals::covered_but_unfed` already draws between a real conserved quantity and a diagnostic that
reveals a problem.

- `PopulationTotals::turned_away: u64` — the running total.
- `Summary::turned_away: u16` — this tick's count, computed the same before/after-totals-diff way
  `born`/`died`/`immigrated`/`emigrated` already are in `tick::summarise`.

Mechanically, `migration::immigration`'s placement loop already stops early once `eligible` runs out —
the events beyond that point are already being dropped, just silently. The change is to count
`events - placed` into `turned_away` instead of discarding it unrecorded.

**Lost, not queued.** Each tick's demand is computed fresh from that tick's population/free-places and
attractiveness, matching the project's existing rule for the granary's lid — "what would go in beyond it
is lost, not queued" — rather than introducing a waiting-list concept that would have to interact with
the `remainder`/`mature()` accumulator.

No new `Event`, no new `RngKind` or `Flow`: `turned_away` is a derived byproduct of the existing
immigration draw, not a flow of its own.

## Out of scope

A real waiting list (queued, not lost) — considered and ruled out above. A distinct `Event` for
sustained turn-aways, for the renderer or the M3 observation layer to react to directly — this phase
adds the counter only; an event on top of it is a natural follow-up once M2/M3 need it, not before. Any
change to `attractiveness`'s own free-places share (the term measuring free places in *served* houses)
— that is a different question from the rate's basis and nothing here asks for it to change.

## Tests

1. **The founding regime reproduces phase 15's proof**: a `hard` city, born with nobody in any house,
   reaches a living population within five years — the same by-eye bar phase 15 closed on
   (`cargo xtask run --difficulty hard`), now produced by the founding regime specifically.
2. **The hand-off happens at the threshold, not before or after**: a city held just under
   `founding_population_threshold` behaves by the founding formula (rate tracks free places, not
   population); once it crosses, the same city's rate starts tracking population instead. Cheapest built
   with the test-only `house_mut` hook phase 15's own test suite already uses to pin a population down
   directly rather than waiting on organic growth to reach the boundary.
3. **A bigger, equally attractive city grows faster**: two cities with the same attractiveness and the
   same free places but different populations draw immigrants at different rates once both are past the
   founding threshold — the property this phase is for.
4. **Zero free places is still zero immigration, in either regime**: phase 15's
   `zero_free_places_is_zero_immigration_and_a_new_house_restarts_it` re-verified; the multiplication by
   `free_places` still gates placement even though it no longer gates the growth-regime rate.
5. **`turned_away` counts exactly the shortfall, and conservation still holds**: a city with high
   attractiveness and little room accumulates `turned_away` while `population_is_conserved_after_any_game`
   (`invariants.rs`) stays green — the proof that the counter really is outside the balance equation and
   not silently duplicating `immigrated`.
6. **The fixture's own migration rates need re-tuning, expect a repeat of phase 15's exercise**: phase
   15's "How it went" already records that a large single rate collided with two short-window coverage
   tests (`on_hard_an_empty_house_is_not_served`, `the_capacity_serves_the_nearest_ones`) that build a
   handful of houses over a dozen ticks and are not about migration at all. Splitting one rate into two
   (founding, growth) plus a threshold reopens that same tuning question and has to be re-verified
   against the same two tests, not assumed safe by analogy.

## Verification

```sh
cargo test -p sim-core migration
PROPTEST_CASES=2000 cargo test -p sim-core --release population_is_conserved_after_any_game
cargo xtask run --difficulty hard --ticks 1800 --dump-every 90
cargo xtask regen-expected
cargo xtask doc-check
```

Same regeneration protocol phase 15 used: green before starting, exactly `minimal`/`hunger` regenerated
and nothing else, idempotent on a second `regen-expected` run, `cargo test -p sim-replay` as the separate
process, and — since the change is entirely inside a table phase 15 already touched — the "point 5"
check has to read the textual dump rather than the hashes, the same way phase 15's own closeout did.

`xtask/src/bench.rs`'s `without_demographics` needs the new founding rate field zeroed too, alongside
the two migration rates it already zeroes, so `--zero-demographics` keeps meaning "nothing moves".

**Done when:** test 3 (population-proportional growth once past the threshold) and test 5
(`turned_away` counted, conservation still exact) pass, and the `hard` by-eye run still reaches a living
city within five years.

## How it went

All of it landed, and the two things the plan got wrong were both found by running the thing rather
than by reading it — one before the first test was written, one only after the tables were tuned.

**The plan's own placement of `turned_away` would have left the headline case reporting zero, and that
was caught before any code was written.** The section above says the change "is to count
`events - placed` instead of discarding it unrecorded", which is true as far as it goes and would have
counted nothing at all in the case the phase exists for. `immigration` returned early on
`free_places == 0`, *before* the rate was computed — so a city that is completely full matured no
events, turned nobody away, and would have reported the same zero as a city nobody wants to live in.
The fix is small and it is the shape the plan's own test 4 already predicted without noticing the
consequence — *"the multiplication by `free_places` still gates **placement** even though it no longer
gates the growth-regime rate"*. The rate is now counted against a **basis**, free places below the
threshold and population at or above it, and the guard is `basis == 0`. In the founding regime that is
byte-for-byte the old behaviour, jitter draw included; in the growth regime the basis is a population
validation keeps at one or more, so events mature with nowhere to go and every one of them is counted.
The knock-on is real and was declared rather than discovered: a full city past the threshold now draws
one more RNG value per tick, and `Flow::Immigration`'s remainder — which is hashed state — moves where
it used to sit at zero.

**Equal rates in the two regimes made the whole phase a no-op, and only the dump said so.** The plan
proposed keeping `immigration_per_thousand_per_month` at phase 15's `30` and letting the new basis do
the work, on an argument written into the table comment: at the hand-off the two regimes agree "exactly
where the city is about half full". The argument is arithmetically right and practically empty. With
both rates equal the growth regime only *overtakes* the founding one once a city is more than half
full, and neither scenario in the tree ever is — houses climb to the top level faster than immigration
fills them, so free places always outnumber residents. Measured across `30`, `60`, `100`, `200` and
`400` on both scenarios: at `30` the population curve barely moved from phase 15's and `turned_away`
stayed at **zero for the whole five years, in both scenarios**, which would have shipped a counter no
existing scenario could reach — the thing `CLAUDE.md`'s scope rule exists to prevent. At `100` a mature
city really does out-draw its own housing: `minimal` on `hard` climbs to 28 residents against
pure-founding's 26, and the counter starts moving where a player can see it. The threshold stayed at
`20`; the sweep showed the `hard` curve climbing through it with no visible step, so the hard cutover
needed no defending after all.

**The threshold's `TooSmall { min: 1 }` check turned out to be load-bearing rather than tidy.** The
plan asked for it by analogy with the other rates. Running the pure-growth variant showed what it
really guards: at a threshold of `1` the founding regime covers a city of nobody and nothing else, and
`minimal` on `hard` crawled to **four** residents in five game years instead of twenty-eight. At `0` it
would cover nothing at all — a population is never below zero — and a city born empty could never draw
a soul. The check is the bootstrap trap written as a table value, and the comment on it now says so.

**Two things the plan expected to fight did not fight.** The fixture's migration rates needed no
delicate re-tuning: the two short-window coverage tests phase 15 collided with are structurally immune
here, and it is worth writing down why rather than leaving it to luck.
`the_capacity_serves_the_nearest_ones` runs on `easy` with every house born full, so `free_places` is
zero and immigration cannot place anybody at any rate; `on_hard_an_empty_house_is_not_served` runs on
`hard` at a population of zero, which is the founding regime doing exactly what it did before this
phase. The founding rate was left at `60` for that second reason, with a comment on the constant saying
not to raise it. The fixture's growth rate went to `200` — the same three-and-a-bit ratio production
ended up with — and the whole suite passed first time.

**The widened `is_off` was checked by running it, not by reading it.** A founding rate left out of
`MigrationRules::is_off` and out of `bench`'s `without_demographics` would not fail any test — it would
quietly let a city under the threshold go on filling while the flag claimed nothing moves, which is the
failure mode `H` exists to avoid and cannot see. `bench --zero-demographics` reports `J = 0%`, no
coverage recomputation in any tick of the run, which is the observable form of nobody having moved.

**`turned_away` needed no change to `population_is_conserved_after_any_game`, and that is the proof
rather than a convenience.** A diagnostic that had quietly crept into the population's arithmetic would
have forced a term into `balance()` and a field into the invariant's failure message. Neither moved,
and the proptest went on passing untouched at 2000 cases. The test file's header now says that out
loud, so the next reader knows the silence is the evidence.

**The four documents.** `RULES.md` **updated**: the Migration section now carries the two regimes, the
turned-away rule and the lost-not-queued rule, and the parameter table and the relations table both
gained rows — `doc-check` refused the commit until the two new parameters were named, which is that
check doing its job. `ROADMAP.md` **updated**: the row moved to *Done* and the status line names 15.5.
`ARCHITECTURE.md` **unaffected, deliberately**: no tick step was added, moved or reordered, no step
changed what it reads or writes, no crate arrived, and no field joined `World` — `population` was
already there and its inner fields are not listed. The draw order is unchanged; immigration's jitter
simply now has something to draw for in one more situation, which is the rule that section already
states. `GLOSSARY.md` **unaffected, deliberately**: "turned away" is plain elementary English and earns
no entry under the naming rule, and the **flow** entry still reads true precisely because
`turned_away` is *not* a fifth flow — which is the same fact that keeps it out of `balance()`.

**The recordings moved for two reasons, both declared in advance**: the dataset hash changed, because
`MigrationRules` gained two fields that `data_hash.rs`'s exhaustive destructure refused to compile
without; and the extra jitter draw changes the RNG sequence in a full city. The protocol was followed
in full — green before starting, exactly `minimal` and `hunger` regenerated and nothing else,
idempotent on the second run, `cargo test -p sim-replay` in its own process. Point 5, the first
diverging tick, had to be read from the textual dump rather than the hashes for the same reason phase
15's closeout did: the dataset hash is part of the state hash, so **every** checkpoint moves from the
first one and the diverging-tick signal is worthless here. What the `.ron` files show instead is
exactly one changed line each, the header's `dataset_hash`, and the population curves before and after
differ in the way the rate change explains and in no other.
