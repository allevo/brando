# Phase 16.9.5 — A map the generator draws can be played

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

**Goal:** a map drawn by today's `gen-map` is committed unedited, and a test loads it through the
replay's own entry point and plays thirty months on it with no command rejected and people still
living there — and every way the generator's output and the runtime still disagree is listed.
**Depends on:** 16.9 — the source-based generator is the only one, under its final name.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

Because the generator's output was reported as not being what the runtime accepts, and nothing in
the tree could say whether that was true. Phase 16.5's own "a drawn map plays" was checked once, by
eye, on `open-plain`, which the removed noise generator drew. The source-based generator's tests
stop at the loader: every map it draws survives `render_map` → `parse_map` → `validate_map`, but no
map it drew has ever been played. `my-map` has been committed since 16.6 and no scenario loads it.

Reading the code predicts that loading cannot be the fault — `save_map` runs the real loader on the
text before anything reaches the disk — so whatever disagrees has to sit around the edges: in how a
map reaches a game, in the header the tool writes, and in what the ground means once it is there.
Playing a drawn map is the way to find out, rather than a way to confirm the prediction.

## What gets built

- **A map.** Drawn by `cargo xtask gen-map` with every flag it requires — 48×32, heights `0..31`,
  16.6's five sources (mountains in the two north corners, seas in the two south corners, land in
  the centre) — from a few seeds into a scratch directory, chosen on the report and the picture, then
  drawn again into `sim-data/maps/` and committed without an edit.
- **A scenario on it**, laid out the way a player would lay one out: a road spine with arms, wells,
  farms and houses, one farm on a slope if the map has one. Named in `NAMES` so `cargo xtask run`
  can show it, and **not** in `RECORDED`, so no recording moves.
- **A test** that records the scenario and hands the recording to `sim_replay::initial_world` — the
  load, the header's map hash and `World::new`, all through the replay's own door — then plays
  thirty months through `sim_core::step`, failing on the first rejected command with the tick, the
  command and the error, and on an empty city at the end.

## Out of scope

**Fixing anything the test or the reading finds.** This task finds the disagreements and lists
them. Which of them to fix, and how, is a decision for the person who reads the list.

**`my-map`.** It stays exactly as it is: it is data, and nothing in this task needs it gone.

## Tests

1. ***The test that defines the phase.*** `a_map_the_generator_drew_loads_and_plays` is green — and
   is watched failing once, with a building moved onto rock, so it is known to be able to fail.
2. ***The test that closes the phase.*** `regen-expected --check` is green before and after: the new
   scenario is not recorded, and nothing reachable from `step()` changes.

## Verification

```sh
cargo xtask regen-expected --check          # before
cargo test -p xtask --bin xtask a_map_the_generator_drew
cargo test --workspace
cargo xtask regen-expected --check          # after: still green, nothing regenerated
cargo xtask doc-check
cargo fmt --all --check
cargo clippy -p map-gen -p xtask --all-targets
cargo xtask run --scenario hills-and-sea --ticks 900 --dump-every 150
```

**Done when:** the committed drawn map is played by the test with no command rejected and people
living on it, and the disagreements are listed below.

## How it went

**Loading and playing a drawn map worked on the first run.** `hills-and-sea` — seed 6, chosen from
six drawn — loads through `initial_world`, and fifteen houses on it reach 113 residents in thirty
months with no command rejected, against 118 for the same kind of city on `open-plain`. The
treasury ends where the layout's costs say it should, slope surcharge included. The test was broken
on purpose by moving a farm onto rock, and failed naming the tick, the command and `WrongTerrain`.
At the level of the file, the generator's output and the runtime do not disagree at all.

**What does disagree** is below. Each one was run, not only read.

- **The runner cannot be pointed at a map.** `run` and `record` take `--scenario` from `NAMES`,
  and a map is reached only through a scenario written in Rust with its coordinates fixed:
  `cargo xtask run --scenario my-map` answers `unknown scenario: my-map`.
- **The header `gen-map` writes is a command `gen-map` refuses.** `note()` records the seed, the
  sources and the id, and leaves out `--width`, `--height`, `--min-height` and `--max-height`, all of
  which `gen-map` requires. Running `hills-and-sea`'s own header answers
  `--width <a number of tiles> is required`.
- **The older headers name commands the tool refuses.** `my-map`'s says `gen-map2`, unknown since
  16.9; `open-plain`'s answers `--min-height <a number> is required`.
- **The runtime's slope rules never act on generated ground, and this is the one reading did not
  predict.** The generator turns every step of 2 or more between land neighbours into rock, so no
  two neighbouring plain tiles differ by more than one, and no 2×2 footprint spans more than a
  slope of 2 — on every seed drawn for this task and on `my-map`. `max_build_slope` is 3, so
  `TooSteep` cannot fire on a drawn map for any footprint in the tables, and `flatten_cost_per_step`
  is paid on at most two steps. `open-plain`, from the noise generator, has footprints at 3 and 4:
  it is the ground those two numbers were tuned on. The generator's rock step and the table's
  `max_build_slope` are two answers to *too steep to build on* that nothing ties together. And
  because a tile the land source claims never moves, the middle of a drawn map is dead level: the
  one farm here that pays for a slope had to reach the edge of the western hills on a spur of its
  own.
- **`--out` and `--id` can put a map where the loader never looks.** `load_map_by_id` reads only
  `sim-data/maps/<id>.ron`, never compares the file's name with the `id` inside it, and the `id` —
  which is also hashed into the map — is not checked as a file name.
- **Water ignores `--min-height`.** It is set to 0 whatever the lower bound: drawn with
  `--min-height 5`, the report says `height 0..31`. The runtime takes 0 without complaint, so this is
  the generator not keeping to its own input rather than the runtime refusing it.

**The plan was wrong about one thing.** It expected to find a farm site on a slope "if the map has
one", as though slope were common. On this generator it is rare, and never more than a step of
one on the sites a city in the middle can reach: that is what turned the fourth disagreement up.

`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `RULES.md` and `GLOSSARY.md` are unaffected: no crate, rule or word changed.
