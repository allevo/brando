# The per-tick recomputation cost

## What the code does

With the services chasing the population, a provider's capacity is counted on the residents
present, so the coverage is invalidated whenever anyone moves. Any change calls
`mark_all_providers_dirty`, and `compute_from_scratch` (`sim-core/src/coverage.rs`) then runs again
for every provider on the map.

## Why it could be a problem

The typical tick goes from 248 µs at the end of M0 to ~3.3 ms — about **13×**. That cost is paid on
every game, and therefore on every batch of automatic balancing, which is the project's second
requirement.

> **Amended 2026-08-17.** One of the two levers has been pulled, and this question stays open on the
> other. [14.9.9](14.9.9-a-provider-stops-looking-once-it-is-full.md) made a provider stop walking
> where its capacity runs out: the empty tick is 2.059 ms against 3.608 ms and `G` is 1.601 ms
> against 3.132 ms, so the 13× above is now about 8×. Nothing about **when** the coverage is
> invalidated changed, which is what the section below is really about and what is left to decide.

> **Amended 2026-08-18, and it is the ground under this question that moved rather than the
> question.** Three phases turned the benchmark's city into knobs —
> [14.9.9.1](14.9.9.1-how-well-served-the-city-is-becomes-a-knob.md) for how many places there are,
> [14.9.9.4](14.9.9.4-a-city-is-founded-at-the-levels-you-ask-for.md) for how big the houses are,
> [14.9.9.5](14.9.9.5-the-streets-stop-being-perfect.md) for whether the streets are a perfect grid —
> and every one of them found the single point this question was going to be answered on to be an
> unrepresentative one:
>
> - **A ragged map costs about 40% more per tick than the lattice**, on the same buildings and *fewer*
>   road tiles, because a missing cross street puts houses further away without moving them. Three
>   seeds agree to within 1%.
> - **The fraction of providers that fill up, which is what the stop is worth, is 90% at one place per
>   resident and 53% at one and a half.** Below one it barely moves: thinning the services changes who
>   goes uncovered, not how full the rest are.
> - **In a city of level-3 houses the stop never fires at all.** A farm's 20 places cannot hold two
>   12-resident houses, so it strands 8 and never reaches zero. The tables are tuned for houses of
>   four.
> - **`A` is far more sensitive to the number of houses than `G` is** — 18% against 5% across the
>   level sweep on an identical set of providers — so much of the tick's cost outside the
>   recomputation scales with the houses, not the providers.
>
> None of that answers the question. What it does is say that an answer taken on the default city
> would have been an answer about a perfect grid full of huts, and there is now an instrument for
> taking it somewhere else.

> **Amended 2026-08-19.** The lever that *was* pulled has now been measured across the axes rather
> than at a point, and what it is worth is a curve: **2.4× where nine providers in ten fill up, 1.3×
> where half of them do, and nothing at all — 0.99×, a coin flip over forty rounds — in a city of
> level-3 houses, where a provider's capacity cannot be exactly consumed and the stop never fires.**
> It holds up on a ragged map (2.1–2.2×). The number to carry forward is therefore not 2.12× but *the
> share of providers that fill up*, which the benchmark now prints, and which predicts the rest.
>
> The re-measurement is in [14.9.9](14.9.9-a-provider-stops-looking-once-it-is-full.md), together with
> a re-run of the tile-at-a-time walk this question's other half keeps circling: still slower, on both
> map kinds, and on a ragged map not even the same simulation.

It was accepted to buy a gameplay dynamic, not through carelessness, and it was left open on purpose
rather than solved during M1. The measurement of step 3 had just shown that the obvious guess about
where its cost lay was wrong — invalidating the coverage only where it changed was the obvious win,
and the condition turned out true on 100% of ticks at the reference scale, so there was nothing to
skip. Optimising in the same phase you measure in means not having the *before*.
