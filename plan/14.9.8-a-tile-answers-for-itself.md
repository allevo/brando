# Phase 14.9.8 — A tile answers for itself

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

**Goal:** a `Tile` exposes no field; it answers questions, and outside `sim-core` it cannot be written
at all.
**Depends on:** nothing. The fields have been public since phase 01.
**Size:** S — accessors, and the call sites the compiler names.

## Why now

`Tile` has three fields and publishes two of them. `occupant_origin` is private, and its comment says
why: it is only meaningful paired with a flag, and a raw read would name the origin of an occupant
already removed. `terrain` and `flags` carry no such trap, so they were left open.

The first thing to say is what this does **not** fix. `World.grid` is `pub(crate)`, `World::grid()`
hands out `&Grid`, and there is no `grid_mut` — so no `&mut Tile` has ever escaped the crate and both
fields are already read-only for every caller outside it. Turning two keywords private changes nothing
an external crate can do. It would be a change made to satisfy a habit.

What it does fix is one line inside the crate. `World::set_terrain` says of itself that it is the only
mutation of the state that does not go through a `Command`, and the sentence is true because nobody has
written a second assignment. `t.terrain = terrain` is an open field, and the claim rests on manners.
Giving it a named door leaves the claim resting on something.

And the door that actually matters is elsewhere: `Grid::get_mut` and `Grid::at_mut` are `pub`. No crate
outside `sim-core` calls either — the tests, `sim-replay` and `xtask` all read tiles and set terrain
through `World::set_terrain`. They are surface held open for nobody, which is the thing the scope rule
in `CLAUDE.md` warns about, and closing them is what makes *a tile is written by a `Command`* a property
of the types instead of an observation about the current callers.

## What gets built

**`Tile` answers, and stores nothing anyone can reach.** All three fields private. `terrain()`,
`has_road()` and `flag_bits()` are `pub`; `set_terrain()`, `set_road()`, `set_occupant()` and
`clear_occupant()` are `pub(crate)`. `occupant()` and `is_free()` are already methods and do not move —
they are why this shape is the right one rather than a new one, since the tile has answered its two
hardest questions this way since phase 01.

**`TileFlags` stops being API and becomes storage.** It leaves the `pub use` in `lib.rs`; no crate
outside `sim-core` ever names the type, they only reach it through the field. Its methods drop to
module-private, their only callers being `Tile`'s methods and the `Debug` impl. The bit constants, the
`set` helper and the `Debug` impl are untouched, and the frozen bit numbers do not move.

**`TileFlags::set_road` is deleted rather than moved down.** This is the load-bearing choice.
`TileFlags` is `Copy`, so had the accessor been `flags() -> TileFlags` with a public mutator behind it,
`t.flags().set_road(true)` would compile and mutate a temporary that is dropped on the next line — a
silent no-op, introduced by a change made for safety, in the one place where a lost write means a road
that was paid for and never laid. `Tile::set_road` goes through the module-private `set` instead, and
the expression that could go wrong no longer parses.

**`Grid::get_mut` and `Grid::at_mut` become `pub(crate)`.** After it, the only ways to write a tile
from outside are `World::set_terrain` at setup and a `Command` during play.

**The comment on `occupant_origin` is rewritten.** It opens with "Private:", which distinguishes it
from its neighbours today and will distinguish it from nothing afterwards. The rule it carries is worth
keeping and has to be restated as a rule about `occupant()` rather than about a keyword.

## Out of scope

**No change to what any of it computes.** No flag gains a meaning, no bit moves, no number is read from
a different place. The recordings are the check that this held.

**No compile-fail test harness.** Proving "this does not compile" wants `trybuild`, which is a
dev-dependency and a new kind of test for one assertion. The suite already proves it a cheaper way, and
the *Tests* section says how.

**Nothing pulled forward from phase 19.** The tile gains no field; the 4-byte budget is untouched and
its test is untouched.

## Tests

**No new test, and the reason is not "it is only a refactor".** There is no new behaviour to pin: every
method added returns exactly what the field access it replaces returned. A test asserting
`tile.terrain() == tile.terrain()` would pin nothing, and the budget test, the frozen-bit-numbers test
and the occupant round-trip already cover the type's real rules.

**What proves the boundary is the suite compiling.** `sim-core/tests/*` are separate crates. They read
tiles, and after this they are compiled against a `Grid` that hands out no `&mut Tile` at all — so if
they build, no write path out of the crate exists. That is a stronger statement than a test could make
and it costs nothing, because those files have to be edited for the accessors anyway.

The existing tests move to the accessors: `grid.rs`'s own unit tests, four assertions in
`tests/commands.rs`, and two in `tests/invariants.rs`. `the_bit_numbers_are_frozen` already goes through
accessors rather than the constants, so it survives with only the receiver changed — which is the
argument that test made for itself when it was written.

## Verification

The hash is the whole point. `hash_world` feeds two bytes per tile, `terrain.index()` and the flag byte;
afterwards it feeds the same two bytes from `terrain()` and `flag_bits()`, in the same order. So
`regen-expected --check` is green before, and green again after with nothing regenerated. A single moved
checkpoint means `flag_bits()` is not returning what `flags.bits()` returned, and that has to be found
before the commit rather than absorbed into it.

The compiler is the proof of completeness. Every field access becomes a hard error, so unlike a rename
carried out by grep, no call site can be missed in silence.

**Done when:** `Tile` has no reachable field and `TileFlags` is not exported; `Grid` hands out no
`&mut Tile` outside `sim-core`; `regen-expected --check` regenerates nothing; and `cargo test
--workspace`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets`, `cargo fmt --all --check` and `cargo xtask
doc-check` are all green.

## How it went

**The surface being closed had no users at all, and that is the finding.** *Why now* argued that
`Grid::get_mut` and `Grid::at_mut` were held open for nobody; the way that claim was settled is worth
recording, because it is the cheapest kind of proof there is. Making the two `pub(crate)` required not
one edit anywhere outside `sim-core/src` — not in the integration tests, not in `sim-replay`, not in
`xtask`. A `pub` whose narrowing costs nothing was reaching nobody, and the compiler says so in a
single build. The same measurement is available for any other `pub` in the tree and takes about a
minute.

**The serde derives were dead, and the plan hedged where it did not need to.** *One conditional step*
was written as an if — remove them if it compiles, keep them and explain if not. It compiled. What the
hedge missed is that the answer was already visible before the change: `Grid` is not `Serialize`, and
a `Tile` that no container serialises cannot be reached by serde from anywhere. The interesting half
is what `Deserialize` was: a second constructor, exempt from every rule the methods enforce, able to
pair an occupant origin with flags that disagree with it. It was not a bug, because nothing called it.
It was the exact hole the rest of the task was closing, sitting in the derive list, and it went
unmentioned in the first two drafts of the plan.

**No hash moved, which is the whole claim.** `regen-expected --check` said `recordings up to date,
nothing to do` before the first edit and again after the last, with both recordings and every
checkpoint in them covered. The bench's own state hash was not compared, and did not need to be: it is
one city at one moment, where the recordings are two runs across every tick, so the stronger check
subsumes it.

**The plan's `## Tests` section made a promise it turned out to be able to keep.** The argument was
that `sim-core/tests/*` are separate crates, so their compiling *is* the proof no write path escapes —
cheaper and stronger than a `trybuild` case. That held exactly, and it is worth keeping as a pattern:
where a boundary is a visibility boundary, an existing external test crate proves it for free, and the
test to write is none.

**One sentence had to be corrected mid-flight, and it is the ordinary failure of writing prose about
code.** The new note on `Tile` first read "deliberately not `Deserialize`, though the fields all are" —
true when it was written and false ten minutes later, because `TileFlags` lost its own derives in the
same change. A sentence that counts what its neighbours do is a second copy of them; the fix was to
stop counting and state the rule about `Tile` alone.

**Running rustdoc turned up three broken intra-doc links, none of them from this change.**
`coverage.rs:5`, `satisfaction.rs:58` and `world.rs:160` fail `RUSTDOCFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo doc`.
They are untouched by this task and pre-date it. The reason they survived is structural rather than
careless: `CLAUDE.md` tells you to link a rule to its code item because "rustdoc then checks the link
for you", and nothing in the definition of done ever runs rustdoc, so the check it promises does not
happen. Left alone here, deliberately — it is a check that does not exist, which is a different piece
of work from a field that is public.

**What did not need doing.** `ARCHITECTURE.md` is consciously declared unaffected: it describes the
4-byte budget and the test guarding it, both untouched, and names no field. `RULES.md` likewise —
it says what a tile carries in game terms and never in field names, which is exactly why a change of
this kind does not reach it. `GLOSSARY.md` gains nothing: `flag_bits` is a name for a byte, not a word
the domain uses, and `terrain`, `road` and `occupant` were already there.
