# Phase 14.9.9.1 — How well served the city is becomes a knob

> Nothing in it is implemented. It is a plan, and the tree may well diverge from it once the
> work is really done — see [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

**Goal:** the benchmark can build a city with more or fewer places than its residents need, so a
measurement of the coverage becomes a curve across that axis instead of a single point.
**Depends on:** 14.9.9.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

[14.9.9](14.9.9-a-provider-stops-looking-once-it-is-full.md) measured a provider that stops walking
once it is full at 2.12× on `G`. That number is true, and it is true of **one city**.

`Layout::new` sizes every kind of provider as `population.div_ceil(capacity)` against the starting
population, so the total number of places is always about equal to the number of residents. The city
it builds therefore sits permanently on a knife-edge: about 5% of residents unserved, and — measured
during 14.9.9 — 64% of providers filling up.

That fraction is exactly what decides what the stop is worth. Build services generously and fewer
providers fill, so the stop fires less often and earns less; build them thinly and more fill, and it
earns more. A player builds neither at exactly one place per resident. So no measurement the
benchmark can take today says how the coverage behaves in the cities people really play, and there is
no way to find out without changing the city it builds.

It matters now rather than later because slot 18.5 has to be **answered** on a measurement of this
kind, and phase 18 promises one. An answer taken at a single point on this axis would be the same
mistake in a more expensive place.

## What gets built

**A knob on the benchmark: `--places <n>`, places per hundred residents, defaulting to 100.** It
multiplies the provider counts `Layout::new` derives from the tables. At 100 it builds exactly the
city it builds today.

*The name.* `places` is what `RULES.md` already calls a unit of a provider's capacity — *a house of
two residents takes two places*, *a provider left with fewer places than the house needs* — so
nothing new is invented and nothing goes in `GLOSSARY.md`. If a plainer phrase turns up while it is
being written, it is free to change.

**The floor stays.** A farm's count is also bounded below by what its output sustains, and that bound
is not a matter of taste: below it, covered houses would go hungry and the consistency between a
producer's capacity and its output would stop holding. So the knob scales the count the capacity
asks for, the production floor still applies, and the header says which of the two bound each kind —
otherwise `--places 50` would silently build the same city as `--places 100` for the farms and the
measurement would be attributed to the wrong thing.

**The header says what city it built.** The share of residents served, and the spread of residents
per house, at the tick the measures start. Without it the knob is a number you set and cannot
confirm, and a reader of a saved benchmark output cannot tell which point on the axis it came from.

## Out of scope

- **Measuring the other difficulty profiles.** `normal` starts a house with two residents instead of
  four, which moves the same axis by a different route and is worth its own look. `hard` cannot be
  measured at all — the benchmark refuses a profile that starts a house empty — and since 14.5 a
  `hard` city has no coverage whatever until migration exists, so it is not measurable until phase 15
  lands. Both are a different task.
- **Making the road lattice sparser or ragged.** The pitch-4 grid roads 45% of the city's footprint,
  with one component and no dead ends, and that also flatters anything that skips road tiles. It is a
  real gap and a separate one: this task changes how many providers there are, not where the roads go.
- **Answering 18.5.** This builds the instrument. The answer is that slot's.

## Tests

The benchmark is not a test and gets no thresholds. What it gets is one check that it still builds
the city it used to:

1. `--places 100` prints the state hash the benchmark prints today, unchanged. It is the whole
   guarantee that the knob was added without moving the ground under every recorded figure.
2. `--places 70` and `--places 150` really build fewer and more providers than 100, and the served
   share moves the way the number says.

## Verification

```sh
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 100    # the hash must not move
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 70
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 150
cargo test --workspace
cargo xtask doc-check
```

**Done when:** `--places 100` prints the same state hash as the benchmark prints today, and 70 and
150 print a served share that moves in the direction they claim — with `G` recorded at all three, so
the next person arguing about the coverage has a curve rather than a point.
