# Phase 14.9.9.1 — How well served the city is becomes a knob

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

> **Amended 2026-08-20.** The tree was renumbered so that terrain could be built next, before the rest
> of M1. Below, "slot 18.5" (the per-tick recomputation cost) is now slot 19.5, and "phase 18" (the
> invariants closeout, which promises the measurement) is now phase 19.

**Goal:** the benchmark can build a city with more or fewer places than its residents need, so a
measurement of the coverage becomes a curve across that axis instead of a single point.
**Depends on:** 14.9.9.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

[14.9.9](14.9.9-a-provider-stops-looking-once-it-is-full.md) measured a provider that stops walking
once it is full at 2.12× on `G`. That number is true, and it is true of **one city**.

`Layout::new` sizes every kind of provider as `population.div_ceil(capacity)` against the starting
population, so the total number of places is always about equal to the number of residents. The city
it builds therefore sits permanently on a knife-edge: about 5% of residents unserved, and — measured
during 14.9.9 — 64% of providers filling up.

That fraction is exactly what decides what the stop is worth. Build services generously and fewer
providers fill, so the stop fires less often and earns less; build them thinly and more fill, and it
earns more. A player builds neither at exactly one place per resident. So no measurement the
benchmark can take today says how the coverage behaves in the cities people really play, and there is
no way to find out without changing the city it builds.

It matters now rather than later because slot 18.5 has to be **answered** on a measurement of this
kind, and phase 18 promises one. An answer taken at a single point on this axis would be the same
mistake in a more expensive place.

## What gets built

**A knob on the benchmark: `--places <n>`, places per hundred residents, defaulting to 100.** It
multiplies the provider counts `Layout::new` derives from the tables. At 100 it builds exactly the
city it builds today.

*The name.* `places` is what `RULES.md` already calls a unit of a provider's capacity — *a house of
two residents takes two places*, *a provider left with fewer places than the house needs* — so
nothing new is invented and nothing goes in `GLOSSARY.md`. If a plainer phrase turns up while it is
being written, it is free to change.

**The floor stays.** A farm's count is also bounded below by what its output sustains, and that bound
is not a matter of taste: below it, covered houses would go hungry and the consistency between a
producer's capacity and its output would stop holding. So the knob scales the count the capacity
asks for, the production floor still applies, and the header says which of the two bound each kind —
otherwise `--places 50` would silently build the same city as `--places 100` for the farms and the
measurement would be attributed to the wrong thing.

**The header says what city it built.** The share of residents served, and the spread of residents
per house, at the tick the measures start. Without it the knob is a number you set and cannot
confirm, and a reader of a saved benchmark output cannot tell which point on the axis it came from.

## Out of scope

- **Measuring the other difficulty profiles.** `normal` starts a house with two residents instead of
  four, which moves the same axis by a different route and is worth its own look. `hard` cannot be
  measured at all — the benchmark refuses a profile that starts a house empty — and since 14.5 a
  `hard` city has no coverage whatever until migration exists, so it is not measurable until phase 15
  lands. Both are a different task.
- **Making the road lattice sparser or ragged.** The pitch-4 grid roads 45% of the city's footprint,
  with one component and no dead ends, and that also flatters anything that skips road tiles. It is a
  real gap and a separate one: this task changes how many providers there are, not where the roads go.
- **Answering 18.5.** This builds the instrument. The answer is that slot's.

## Tests

The benchmark is not a test and gets no thresholds. What it gets is one check that it still builds
the city it used to:

1. `--places 100` prints the state hash the benchmark prints today, unchanged. It is the whole
   guarantee that the knob was added without moving the ground under every recorded figure.
2. `--places 70` and `--places 150` really build fewer and more providers than 100, and the served
   share moves the way the number says.

## Verification

```sh
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 100    # the hash must not move
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 70
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --places 150
cargo test --workspace
cargo xtask doc-check
```

**Done when:** `--places 100` prints the same state hash as the benchmark prints today, and 70 and
150 print a served share that moves in the direction they claim — with `G` recorded at all three, so
the next person arguing about the coverage has a curve rather than a point.

## How it went

**The knob became one per kind of provider before a line of it was written, and the tables are the
reason.** The plan proposed `--places <n>` for every provider at once. But `farm` declares a capacity
of 20 residents and an output that sustains exactly 20, so its two bounds are the same number and
every setting below the neutral one leaves the farms precisely where they were: `--places 70` builds
329 wells against 469, and 750 farms against 750. The plan named that as a risk to be guarded against
with a header line. It is not a risk, it is what every run below 100 does. So the flag takes
`<kind>=<n>` as well as a bare number, and the two compose — `--places 70,farm=150` reads as
"everything thin, except the farms". Not `--well 70`: `buildings.ron` says outright that a roster of
building kinds written into the code is what D6 forbids, and the benchmark already finds its kinds by
iterating the table.

**`--tables <dir>` was asked for while this was being planned, and turned out to be five lines rather
than a subsystem.** `sim_data::load_from_dir` is already public and already runs the whole validation,
so pointing the benchmark at an edited copy of `sim-data/data` reaches every number the game has
without one line of per-field plumbing — and the numbers stay in RON, where D6 wants them. An
inconsistent copy is refused by the core's own check, naming the field: raising the farm's capacity to
21 gets *"capacity of 21 residents, but the output sustains 20"*. The trade is that sweeping a table
value means generating a directory in a shell loop, which is cheaper than writing and keeping a typed
path-setter over `DataSet`.

**The spread of residents per house cannot exist at the tick the measures start, and the plan asked
for it anyway.** `build` founds every house at the profile's occupancy, and the first birth needs a
month boundary that measure `A` has not reached — so the figure is the same single number on every
run, at every setting of every knob. Its slot went to `houses per level`, which is the same shape of
fact and is degenerate today for the same reason. The knob that fills it is the next task.

**One share per service, and not one share of "whatever its own level asks for".** The first house
level is a hut that requires water only, on purpose, so the first rung is reachable before there is a
farm. Every house in the synthetic city stands at level 1, so a single figure read off the levels'
requirements would have been a pure water number with the farms invisible — at `--places 150` the
farms grow by half and it would not have moved at all. The header prints both, and the gap between
them is the tell.

**The fraction of providers that fill up needs nothing inside the core.** A provider stops the moment
its capacity reaches zero, so one that stopped has consumed its capacity exactly: summing residents
per serving provider over `Coverage::served_by`, which is behind no feature, and comparing against the
kind's declared capacity says which ones filled. No counter in `sim-core`, and no `test-util` — which
`sim-core/Cargo.toml` refuses to xtask by name.

**The hash guarantee is tested as arithmetic, not as a recorded constant.** That `--places 100` prints
today's hash is a fact about one dataset on one day; `ceil(a·k / b·k) == ceil(a/b)` is *why* it is
true, and it stays true when the tables move. A recorded hash would have had to be regenerated on
every balance change and would by then have been proving nothing.

**The measurements.** `--reps 100`, 200×200, 15,000 residents, one machine, one sitting:

| `--places` | wells | farms | providers | full | water covered | `A` empty tick | `G` step 3 | `G` per provider |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70 | 329 | 750 | 1,079 | 91% | 70% | 2.074 ms | 1.689 ms | 1,565 ns |
| 100 | 469 | 750 | 1,219 | 90% | 97% | 2.139 ms | 1.650 ms | 1,353 ns |
| 150 | 704 | 1,125 | 1,829 | 53% | 99% | 3.299 ms | 2.567 ms | 1,403 ns |

**The axis is one-sided, and nobody predicted that.** Thinning the services does not make the city
more contended: 91% of providers fill at 70 places against 90% at 100. What changes below the neutral
setting is who goes **uncovered** — water reaches 70% of residents instead of 97% — while the
providers that remain fill just as readily, because the houses they lost were never the binding
constraint. The fraction only moves going the other way, and there it moves a long way: at 150 places
barely half of them fill. So the quantity a provider's stop is worth resting on lives in the
**generous** half of the axis, and the single point 14.9.9 measured sits at the end of the range where
it has already saturated.

**`G` is markedly sub-linear in the number of providers.** From 70 to 150 places the providers go up
1.70× and `G` 1.52×; per provider the cost is *worst* at 70 places, 1,565 ns against 1,353 at the
neutral setting. Both say the same thing: a provider in a thinly served city walks further before it
fills, so taking providers away does not give back all of what they cost. That is the shape slot 18.5
has to be answered on, and none of it was visible from one point.

**The header's `full` reads 90% at the default where 14.9.9's instrumented run reported 64%, and the
two are not the same measurement.** This one is taken at the tick the measures start; that one was
taken inside a recomputation some hundreds of ticks in, on a city whose unserved houses had been
emptying the whole time. It is the trap that phase's own amendment records — *a rule change cannot be
timed on `G`* — turning up in a second guise, and it is why this header is printed beside the state
hash it describes rather than anywhere else.

**Both state hashes are untouched at the default**, `c40b8f6546727f67` and `758179a5698f46dc`, and
`regen-expected --check` was green before the work and after it.

**One repair in the diff that is not this task's.** `cargo fmt --all` had drifted in `coverage.rs`'s
test module, in code this phase never touched. It is two hunks, and it is here because the closing
checklist asks for `--check` to be clean and leaving it would mean carrying a red check forward.

**`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `RULES.md` and `GLOSSARY.md` are unaffected, and each was looked at.** No tick
step moved and no field joined `World`. No game rule changed, and `RULES.md` already calls a unit of a
provider's capacity a *place*, which is where the flag's name comes from. No hard word was invented,
and `GLOSSARY.md`'s row on the benchmark's labels still describes them all — this task added facts to
the header, not a lettered measure. `ROADMAP.md` gained this row.
