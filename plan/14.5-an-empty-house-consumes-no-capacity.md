# An empty house consumes no capacity

## What the code does

`pick_within_capacity` (`sim-core/src/coverage.rs`) walks the candidates in priority order and
subtracts each one's residents from what is left:

```rust
left = left.checked_sub(residents)?;      // residents == 0 always succeeds
```

In `hard` profile, every new house is born with `starting_residents_per_house: 0`. So you create houses
without any resident. Therefore the subtraction never fails, so a provider takes every house in range whatever its capacity.

## Why it could be a problem

An emptied house keeps its coverage, so its satisfaction climbs with nobody in it and it can be
promoted at the monthly review. Nothing breaks while it lasts — a promotion grants permission and
not people — but it leaves a house that is empty, served, and standing at a level it never
earned by housing anyone.

Promotion needs a well and a farm, and because empty 
houses weigh nothing against either provider's capacity, one well plus one farm — each sized for a
handful of residents — carry an unbounded number of empty houses to the top level.

## The answer

**A house with nobody in it is not served.** A service exists to reach residents, so a house with
none is no candidate for one, at any distance, for any provider. A house emptied by deaths loses its
coverage, and gets it back when somebody moves in.

Two other answers were on the table, and the argument for this one is what it *keeps*:

- **An empty house takes one place** — `left.checked_sub(residents.max(1))`. It bounds the damage,
  since a well declared for thirty-two residents then carries at most thirty-two empty houses. But
  the sentence *capacity is counted in residents, not in houses* gains an exception the first time
  anybody reads the code, and empty houses go on competing with occupied ones for places they cannot
  use.
- **Accept it.** The reason not to is in the section above, and it did not get weaker: coverage feeds
  satisfaction, and satisfaction is the whole of what a level-up reads.

Not serving them keeps the capacity sentence exactly true, and it answers the promotion problem at
the root rather than bounding it: an empty house gathers no satisfaction because it is served by
nobody, so there is nothing to promote it on.

**What this hands to phase 15, and it is a constraint and not a note.** Immigration must not make
coverage a precondition for moving into a particular house. Coverage drives the *rate* at which a
city attracts people, which is what phase 15 is about; if it also decided *which* house they enter,
an emptied house could never be refilled — uncovered because empty, empty because uncovered. That is
an absorbing state, and this project has already been bitten once by one.

The rule is stated in full where it binds, in the comment on the candidate filter in
`sim-core/src/coverage.rs`, and in `RULES.md`'s coverage section. The two tests that pinned the old
behaviour were written to change their outcome rather than break, and they did:
`on_hard_an_empty_house_is_not_served` and `an_emptied_house_loses_its_coverage`.

**The recordings did not move, and that was not the expectation.** Both recorded scenarios were
thought to carry the old behaviour, on the grounds that deaths reach zero residents on every
profile. They run 360 ticks, and no house in either of them empties inside that: `regen-expected
--check` stayed green across the rule change, with nothing regenerated. The comment on
`an_emptied_house_loses_its_coverage` claimed the opposite and has been corrected.

**One test elsewhere was resting on the old rule without saying so.**
`the_level_shows_no_oscillation_with_constant_services` (`sim-core/tests/levels.rs`) went red: its
corner house was emptied by deaths at tick 91 and fell from level 3 to level 2. Its premise — *the
city is laid out, no command is issued, so nothing but the levels themselves can move* — stopped
being true when phase 14 gave the population a life of its own, and the old rule was the only thing
hiding it, because an empty house went on being served and its satisfaction never fell. It now runs
on the fixture with the demographics switched off, which is what the sentence in its own doc comment
always described.
