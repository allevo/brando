# Phase 14.9.9 — A provider stops looking once it is full

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

**Goal:** a provider whose capacity has run out stops walking the roads, and serves exactly whom it
served before.
**Depends on:** 14.5, whose answer is what makes stopping sound at all.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

Step 3 is the hot path and slot 18.5 records what it costs: the typical tick went from 248 µs at the
end of M0 to ~3.3 ms, and `G` says 3.05 ms of that is the recomputation, on 100% of ticks.

Inside it every provider walks to its full `range_per_level` and collects every house it can reach,
then throws nearly all of them away. `bfs_roads` stops on `d == max` or an empty frontier and on
nothing else: the capacity is counted afterwards, over the sorted candidate list, so nothing in the
walk knows the provider filled up five tiles ago. A well declares 32 residents and houses hold four,
so it serves eight — and range 12 reaches far more than eight.

This is the largest lever on step 3 that does not touch **when** the coverage is invalidated, which
is the other half of 18.5 and a different question.

**It could not be built before 14.5.** The stop can only fire at `left == 0`, because any positive
remainder might still fit a smaller house further out — *whoever does not fit is skipped, and does
not act as a barrier* is a rule with a table test behind it. And `left == 0` was not a safe place to
stop while `left.checked_sub(0)` succeeded: a house with nobody in it was served for free at any
distance. Now that a house with nobody in it is not a candidate, every candidate weighs at least one
resident, and nothing fits into nothing.

## What gets built

**`bfs_roads` hands over a whole distance at a time, and takes an answer back.** The callback becomes
`FnMut(&[TileIndex], u16) -> bool`, called once per distance in increasing order, tiles within a call
in increasing `TileIndex`. Returning `false` ends the walk there and the tiles beyond are never
marked.

A distance and not a tile, and it is not a matter of taste: at equal distance the priority order is
the **house's** origin tile, not the road tile it was reached from, and those two orders are
different whenever one house faces its street from the north and another from the south. A per-tile
callback that filled as it went would settle a contested house on which side of the street it stood.

**`World::road_distance` gets the same stop for free** — the first distance holding a target is the
answer, because the walk goes outwards.

**The block below the walk in `compute_from_scratch` moves inside it.** Per distance: collect that
distance's fresh, free, inhabited houses into a reused buffer keyed by the house's origin tile, sort
it, fill from it. `left` is carried across the distances by `pick_within_capacity`, which now takes
it by reference, and the callback answers `left > 0`. The whole-range candidate vector and the
per-provider `Vec<HouseId>` of picks both go.

**An oracle, because the usual one goes blind here.** `coverage_equivalence` holds the stored
coverage against `Coverage::from_scratch`, and both of those stop: a stop that ended one distance too
early would give the same wrong answer on either side and stay green — the same blindness the comment
on `Visited` warns about. So `compute_from_scratch` takes a flag, and `Coverage` gains, under
`test-util`, a `from_scratch_walking_the_whole_range` that does not stop.

## Out of scope

- **Reusing the scratch across ticks.** `Visited`, `HousesByTile` and the per-provider vectors are
  allocated afresh on every recomputation. Its own slot; on phase 09's numbers it is a few percent,
  not a multiple.
- **`Visited` as a bitset with an explicit clear** instead of a map-sized epoch array. It might well
  pay in cache, but this phase cuts the tiles touched by half first, so measuring it before would be
  measuring the wrong thing.
- **Making the coverage incremental, or invalidating it less often.** That is 18.5.

## Tests

1. `stopping_at_a_distance_walks_no_further` (`network.rs`) — a stop gives exactly what a walk cut
   off there gives, **and** no tile beyond it is even marked. The second half is the one worth
   having: a stop that merely hid the far tiles from the caller would pass the first and buy nothing.
2. `at_equal_distance_the_order_is_the_houses_and_not_the_road_tiles` — one house faces its street
   from below and one from above, so the road-tile order and the house order disagree and the answer
   says which one the game uses.
3. `stopping_when_full_serves_the_same_houses_as_walking_the_whole_range` — the property test against
   the exhaustive walk.
4. `pick_within_capacity`'s table test gains a second pass that feeds it the same candidates one at a
   time, as the walk really does.

## Verification

```sh
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench          # before and after, same machine
cargo test --workspace
PROPTEST_CASES=1000 cargo test -p sim-core --release coverage_equivalence
cargo test -p sim-replay
cargo xtask regen-expected --check
cargo xtask doc-check
```

**Done when:** `G` has dropped, and **no hash has moved** — `regen-expected --check` green without
regenerating anything, and the state hash the benchmark prints identical before and after. A hash
that shifts here is the optimisation having changed the semantics, and it is a bug.

## How it went

**The walk was measured before it was changed, in a throwaway worktree, and the measurement moved the
expectation a long way.** The guess in the conversation that opened this was that a provider fills up
at about distance 4 of 12, which — the walk growing roughly with the square of the distance — would
have been something like nine times the work it needed. It is not what happens. One recomputation at
the reference scale, instrumented:

| | 100×100, 3,000 res. | 200×200, 15,000 res. |
|---|---|---|
| providers walked | 244 | 1,219 |
| of those, filled up | 173 (70%) | 789 (64%) |
| road tiles visited | 27,829 | 146,341 |
| tiles up to the fill | 13,307 (47%) | 78,658 (53%) |
| candidates collected | 17,177 | 92,415 |
| candidates served | 1,466 (8%) | 7,261 (7%) |
| mean range | 10.8 | 10.8 |
| mean distance walked to | 6.3 | 6.8 |

**A third of the providers never fill up at all**, and the ones that do get most of the way to their
range first. Contention is why: the providers are sized so that their total capacity is about the
population, so once the ones before have taken the houses nearby, the ones after have to go looking.
So the stop was worth a little under half the walk, not nine tenths of it — still far above the
abort condition this phase was given, and the measurement is the only reason that was a fact rather
than a hope.

**It then paid about twice what the tiles alone predicted.** Same machine, same sitting,
`--reps 100`:

| | 100×100 before | after | | 200×200 before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A` empty tick | 733 µs | 406 µs | 1.80× | 3.608 ms | 2.059 ms | 1.75× |
| `D` 1 accepted command | 732 µs | 378 µs | 1.94× | 3.509 ms | 1.955 ms | 1.79× |
| `F` 1 road | 759 µs | 398 µs | 1.91× | 3.581 ms | 2.050 ms | 1.75× |
| `G` step 3 alone | 676 µs | 329 µs | **2.06×** | 3.132 ms | 1.601 ms | **1.96×** |

`G` per provider went 2,770 → 1,346 ns at 100×100 and 2,570 → 1,313 ns at 200×200, so the flatness
phase 09 valued survives. Cutting 47% of the tiles gave nearly 2× because the restructure took three
other things with it: the candidates beyond the fill are never collected either, one sort over the
whole range became several small ones, and the `Vec` of picks allocated once per provider is gone.

> **Amended 2026-08-17, and the table above credits the wrong thing.** Its "before" was taken on the
> tree as it stood before **both** 14.5 and this phase, so what it measures is the pair. Twenty
> paired rounds across the three commits separate them:
>
> | | original | 14.5 only | 14.5 + the stop |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | `G` step 3 alone | 3219.5 µs | 3393.0 µs | 1600.0 µs |
> | `A` empty tick | 3529.5 µs | 3528.5 µs | 2064.0 µs |
>
> **The stop on its own is 2.12× on `G` and 1.71× on the empty tick** — slightly better than the
> combined figure, because 14.5 was working mildly against it. **14.5 on its own is worth nothing:**
> exactly 0 µs on the empty tick, 95% CI [−7, +5], and 5.3% *worse* on `G`. That last number is not
> a slower algorithm, it is a different city: `G` is taken after some 800 ticks inside the same
> process, and under 14.5 an emptied house loses its coverage, decays, and changes the level mix the
> coverage is then computed over. The empty tick starts from an identical state and shows the truth.
> The lesson for the next measurement: **a rule change cannot be timed on `G`**, only on `A`.

**Both state hashes are untouched and nothing was regenerated**, which is what this phase's
done-when asked for.

**The oracle was toothless when first written, and only a deliberately broken stop showed it.**
Written with the generator `coverage_equivalence` uses — roads and buildings scattered over the same
window at random — it stayed green against a stop rigged to end after the very first distance. What
comes out of that generator is mostly buildings with no road beside them, and a provider that reaches
nobody past its own doorstep exercises no stop at all. It now lays two streets first and builds only
on the plots along them. **The lesson is the one worth carrying:** a test written to catch a class of
bug should be run against a member of that class before it is believed.

**The same probe found a second gap, in a test that predates this phase.**
`at_equal_distance_the_smaller_tile_wins` puts both its houses on the same side of the street, so the
road-tile order and the house order agree and either rule gives its answer. Deleting the
within-distance sort altogether broke nothing. That was survivable while the sort ran once over the
whole range; it is not now that the ordering is what justifies handing over a distance at a time, so
a second case was added where the two orders disagree.

**One prediction in the plan for 14.5 was wrong in a way worth keeping.** It said the recordings would
move and this phase would leave them alone. The recordings never moved at all: neither scenario runs
long enough — 360 ticks — for a house to empty.

> **Amended 2026-08-17.** The obvious follow-on was measured straight afterwards and is **slower**.
> If the tie-break at equal distance did not have to be *the house's own tile*, nothing would have to
> be gathered before being served: the callback could go back to one tile at a time, the buffer and
> the per-distance sort would go, and the walk could stop mid-distance instead of at the end of one.
> Three versions of it were built — ordering the houses of one road tile by the house's own tile, by
> its id, and not at all — and run against the committed code in **forty interleaved rounds**, all
> four back to back inside each round with the order rotated, so the machine's drift is common to
> them and cancels in the per-round differences. Medians of those differences, with a bootstrap
> interval and how many of the forty rounds the variant won:
>
> | | `A` empty tick | | | `G` step 3 alone | | |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | | median Δ | 95% CI | won | median Δ | 95% CI | won |
> | road tile, then house tile | **+40.5 µs** (+2.0%) | [+37, +42] | 0/40 | −6.0 µs (−0.4%) | [−8, −1] | 27/38 |
> | road tile, then house id | **+37.0 µs** (+1.8%) | [+34, +38] | 0/40 | −8.5 µs (−0.5%) | [−10, −5.5] | 31/38 |
> | no second order | +12.0 µs (+0.6%) | [+9.5, +16] | 2/40 | +14.5 µs (+0.9%) | [+12, +19.5] | 2/39 |
>
> **The drift in the committed variant alone across the forty rounds was 6.4%**, thirteen times the
> largest effect, and an earlier three-round look at the same question concluded "no difference" —
> which was under-powered rather than right. The differences are real and they are all under two
> percent, and none of the three is faster on `A`, which is the number paid on every tick. The first
> two even *lose* in all forty rounds while winning narrowly inside `G`, and part of that is this
> throwaway's own doing — its group sort walks all 40,001 window pairs of `offsets` per
> recomputation, which a real implementation would not — but the third does no ordering work at all
> and is slower too.
>
> Only the first two are like-for-like: they print the same state hash as the committed code, so they
> run the identical simulation. The third is a different rule doing different work. One caveat on
> `G` either way: it is taken after some 800 ticks inside the same process, so the city it runs on
> has evolved under each variant's own rule, which is the other reason to weight `A` more.
>
> So the estimate that opened this — 10% to 25% — was wrong in the same direction as the two before
> it, and this time the measurement stopped a change rather than sizing one. It would have bought no
> speed, regenerated every recording, and in two of the three shapes traded a tie-break that is a
> function of the map for one that depends on the order the city was built in or on two
> implementation details documented as deliberately unpromised. The reason for the shape is now in
> the comment on `bfs_roads`, so the next reader finds the measurement rather than the idea.

> **Amended 2026-08-19, after the knobs of 14.9.9.1, 14.9.9.4 and 14.9.9.5 made it possible to ask
> both of this phase's questions on a city that is not a perfect grid full of huts. Both answers
> hold. Two of the things said around them do not.**
>
> **What the stop is worth, re-measured a better way.** The 2.12× above came from twenty paired
> rounds *across three commits*, on `G`, which this phase's own lesson says is taken on a drifted
> city. It can now be measured without any of that: `Coverage::from_scratch` against
> `from_scratch_walking_the_whole_range` — the `test-util` oracle, the same walk with the stop taken
> out — inside one process, on one city, at the tick the measures start, both timed in the same round
> with the order rotated. Sixty rounds each, 200×200, 15,000 residents:
>
> | city | providers full | with the stop | without | worth |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | the lattice | 90% | 1.079 ms | 2.606 ms | **2.41×**, 60/60 rounds |
> | seeded map 1 | 82% | 1.711 ms | 3.736 ms | 2.18×, 60/60 |
> | seeded map 2 | 82% | 1.710 ms | 3.807 ms | 2.22×, 60/60 |
> | seeded map 3 | 81% | 1.785 ms | 3.749 ms | 2.10×, 60/60 |
>
> So the stop survives a ragged map, and 2.12× was if anything an underestimate of it on the lattice.
>
> **But what it is worth is not a number, it is a function of how many providers fill up** — which is
> the quantity 14.9.9.1 put in the benchmark's header, and it turns out to predict this almost
> exactly:
>
> | knob | providers full | the stop is worth |
> |---|---|---|
> | `--places 70` | 91% | 2.74× |
> | `--places 100` | 90% | 2.42× |
> | `--places 150` | 53% | 1.29× |
> | `--houses 2=1875` | 21% | 1.33× |
> | `--houses 3=1250` | 0% | **0.99×, saved on 20 of 40 rounds** |
>
> The last row is a coin flip, which is the arithmetic showing through: a farm's twenty places cannot
> hold two twelve-resident houses, so `left` never reaches zero, so the stop never fires. **In a city
> of large houses this phase bought nothing at all.** Nothing here says the change was wrong — it is
> paid for many times over in the cities the game actually starts in — only that its value was
> measured at the end of the range where it is largest.
>
> **The tile-at-a-time walk, rebuilt and re-run.** Three variants again — the houses of one road tile
> ordered by the house's own tile, by its id, and not at all — but implemented afresh, and without the
> handicap the last one carried: this one sorts at most four houses per road tile instead of walking
> all 40,001 window pairs of `offsets`. Forty interleaved rounds, four binaries back to back per round
> with the order rotated, `--reps 30`, medians of the paired differences with a 95% bootstrap
> interval. Positive is slower than the committed code:
>
> | | `A` on the lattice | `G` on the lattice | `A` on a seeded map | `G` on a seeded map |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | road tile, then house tile | +6.78% [+128, +153] µs | +11.05% [+182, +213] µs | +2.95% [+57, +115] µs | +3.80% [+70, +131] µs |
> | road tile, then house id | +4.67% [+82, +104] µs | +8.25% [+126, +164] µs | +2.21% [+33, +81] µs | +4.11% [+93, +115] µs |
> | no second order | +3.98% [+69, +97] µs | +4.68% [+62, +93] µs | +2.39% [+51, +98] µs | +1.59% [+1, +70] µs |
>
> **The conclusion holds on both maps and it is not close.** A ragged map narrows the gap — the walk
> spends more of itself on the roads and less on the houses, so the part these variants change matters
> less — but it never reverses it.
>
> **Where the record above was wrong: "only the first two are like-for-like" is true of the lattice
> and of nothing else.** They print the committed hash on the default city, which is what made that
> sentence look like a property of the rules. It is a property of a uniform city on a perfect grid:
>
> | city | road tile, then house tile | road tile, then house id | no second order |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | lattice, all houses level 1 | same hash | same hash | differs |
> | lattice, all houses level 2 | differs | differs | differs |
> | seeded map | differs | differs | differs |
>
> Away from that one city all three compute a **different coverage**, so on a ragged map the timings
> above are not like-for-like either and are quoted as indicative. That strengthens the original
> refusal rather than weakening it: the committed order is a function of the map, and every variant
> makes the answer depend on the order the walk happens to reach the road tiles in.
>
> **The two implementations disagree about `G` and agree about `A`.** The first found the first two
> variants winning narrowly inside `G` (−0.4%, −0.5%) and blamed its own group sort; this one has them
> losing by 8% and 11%, and its own overhead is the mirror image — a call into
> `pick_within_capacity` per road tile instead of per distance. Neither `G` figure is a property of
> the *idea*, only of a build of it. Both agree on `A`, in all eighty rounds across the two sittings,
> and `A` is what is paid on every tick. **The lesson is not about this walk:** where two honest
> implementations of one idea disagree on the sign of a 1% effect, the effect belongs to the
> implementations and the question needs a measure that does not.

**`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `RULES.md` and `GLOSSARY.md` are unaffected, and each was looked at.** The rule
did not change: a provider serves exactly whom it served, and `RULES.md` describes what the game
does, not how far it walks to do it. `coverage.rs` is still the hot path and `network.rs` still holds
walked distances. No word changed meaning. `ROADMAP.md` gained this row, and slot 18.5 gained a
dated amendment saying one of its levers has been pulled and roughly what is left.
