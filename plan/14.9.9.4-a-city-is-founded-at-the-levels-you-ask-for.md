# Phase 14.9.9.4 — A city is founded at the levels you ask for

> See [How it went](#how-it-went) at the end, which is where the shape it really took is written
> down. Everything above that section is the plan as it was written, including the parts the work
> proved wrong — the prediction next to the outcome is the point.

> **Amended 2026-08-20.** The tree was renumbered so that terrain could be built next, before the rest
> of M1. Below, "slot 18.5" (the per-tick recomputation cost) is now slot 19.5.

**Goal:** the benchmark builds a city whose houses are not all at the first level, so the coverage can
be measured against the size of the houses it has to fit and not only against how many places there
are.
**Depends on:** 14.9.9.1, which is where `--places` and the header come from.
**Size:** S.

## Why now

[14.9.9.1](14.9.9.1-how-well-served-the-city-is-becomes-a-knob.md) made how well served the city is a
knob, and found the axis one-sided: below the neutral setting the providers that remain fill just as
readily, and only who goes uncovered changes. It could not look at the other half of the question,
because every house it builds is the same size.

Capacity is counted in residents and handed out in whole houses — *a provider left with fewer places
than the house needs leaves it standing* — so how much of a provider's capacity is reachable at all
depends on how big the houses are. A city of huts and a city of villas with the same population are
not the same load, and the benchmark could only build the first.

It also leaves half the header unreadable. The first house level is a hut that requires water only, on
purpose, so a figure read off what each house's own level asks for is a pure water number while every
house stands at level 1 — the farms cannot appear in it at all.

## What gets built

**`--houses <level>=<n>,...`**, houses to found at each level: `--houses 1=2000,2=800,3=200`. It
replaces `--residents`, which says the same thing in the one spelling the tables already allow, and
giving both is an error rather than a precedence rule nobody would remember.

**A house founded at a level is founded full**, holding that level's `max_residents`. A level and an
occupancy that could disagree would be two knobs where the game has one, and what a house built
*during* play starts with is already the difficulty profile's to say.

**A setup hook in the core, behind its own feature.** A house is placed at the first level with the
difficulty's occupancy, one value for the whole world, so no dataset can produce a mixed city.
`house_mut` would do it and is behind `test-util`, which `sim-core/Cargo.toml` refuses to `xtask` by
name — it would compile the direct-mutation hooks into every plain workspace build. So a new narrow
feature, `house-level`, exposing `World::set_house_level` and nothing else, which is the pattern
`from-scratch` and `tick-number` already set.

The levels are scattered across the city rather than laid down in runs, for the reason `Spread`
already gives: bunching changes the shape of the load on the walk.

## Out of scope

- **Founding a house at a level with an occupancy of its own.** Two knobs where the game has one.
- **Anything about where the roads go.** Still 14.9.9.1's gap, and still a separate task.
- **Rebalancing the capacities.** If it turns out the tables fit one house size and not another, that
  is a finding this task reports and not one it acts on.

## Tests

1. `set_house_level` founds the house full for its level and invalidates the coverage; a level the
   tables do not have is refused and leaves the house exactly as it was.
2. The histogram founds exactly the city it names — to the house, at every level — and the levels come
   out scattered rather than in runs.
3. A level past the end of the table, and level 0, are refused where they are asked for.
4. The default run, with no `--houses`, still prints the state hash it printed before.

## Verification

```sh
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench                       # the hash must not move
cargo run --release -p xtask -- bench --houses 1=2000,2=800,3=200
cargo test --workspace
cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings && cargo fmt --all --check
cargo xtask regen-expected --check
cargo xtask doc-check
```

**Done when:** the default run's state hash is unchanged, a mixed city reports a level distribution
that matches the flag to the house, and the share of residents whose own level is satisfied really
falls below the water share — which is the farms becoming visible.

## How it went

**The default run is untouched and the two spellings of the same city are not the same state**, and
the plan for this predicted the opposite. `--houses 1=3750` and `--residents 15000` describe an
identical city — 3750 houses, all at level 1, all holding four — but they do not print the same hash,
because founding a level has to be followed by a coverage recomputation and a recomputation costs a
tick. A tick is state. So the histogram path lays the houses, founds their levels, and only then
places the providers, which is one tick more than the default path and buys a city with no
transitional state in it: the first coverage ever computed is computed over the occupancies the run
really has. The default takes the old branch and places everything at once, which is what keeps
`c40b8f6546727f67` and `758179a5698f46dc` where they were.

**The claim worth testing turned out not to be the hash anyway.** What the histogram has to get right
is the city, and that is asserted directly: 150 houses at `1=100,2=40,3=10` come out as exactly
100/40/10, and the population is exactly what those levels hold.

**The measurements.** `--reps 100`, 200×200, the same 15,000 residents each time, packed into fewer
and larger houses. The provider counts are identical in all four — the same 469 wells and 750 farms —
because the population is the same:

| `--houses` | houses | water | food | own level's needs | providers full | `A` empty tick | `G` step 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1=3750` | 3,750 | 97% | 95% | 97% | 90% | 2.080 ms | 1.638 ms |
| `1=1875,2=938` | 2,813 | 85% | 84% | 77% | 80% | 1.966 ms | 1.636 ms |
| `2=1875` | 1,875 | 60% | 48% | 30% | 21% | 1.802 ms | 1.567 ms |
| `3=1250` | 1,250 | 33% | 27% | 10% | **0%** | 1.713 ms | 1.552 ms |

**The provider stop does not fire even once in a city of level-3 houses, and that is arithmetic.**
Capacity is handed out in whole houses, so what a provider can actually give away is
`floor(capacity / residents_per_house) × residents_per_house`:

| level | residents | well, 32 places | farm, 20 places |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 8 houses, 0 stranded | 5 houses, 0 stranded |
| 2 | 8 | 4 houses, 0 stranded | 2 houses, 4 stranded |
| 3 | 12 | 2 houses, 8 stranded | 1 house, 8 stranded |

At level 3 neither kind can ever reach zero places left, so `left > 0` stays true, so every provider
walks its whole range and the stop that [14.9.9](14.9.9-a-provider-stops-looking-once-it-is-full.md)
measured at 2.12× is worth exactly nothing. **The tables are tuned for houses of four**, and the
stranding is not a bug in the coverage — *whoever does not fit is skipped, and does not act as a
barrier* is a rule with a table test behind it — but it is a fact about the balancing that nobody had
seen, and it says the answer to slot 18.5 cannot be taken on a city of huts.

**The level sweep confounds two things, and the honest reading has to say so.** Holding the farms at
750 holds the city at 750 blocks, because a 2x2 provider takes a block of its own, while the houses
that fill the rings fall from 3,750 to 1,250. So a city of villas is also a **sparser** city, and part
of the collapse in coverage is range rather than capacity: at level 3 the fragmentation ceiling above
allows 75% water and 60% food, and the run measures 33% and 27%. Both effects are real and this
benchmark cannot yet separate them. Separating them is the map's business, and the map is the next
task.

**`A` falls by 18% across the sweep while `G` falls by 5%**, on an identical set of providers. What
changes is the number of houses, so most of the tick's sensitivity to how many houses there are lives
*outside* the coverage recomputation — in the steps that walk the houses themselves. That is a
decomposition slot 18.5 asked for and it was not available before.

**`preset` grew an eighth parameter and clippy refused it**, which was the right refusal: the knobs
travel together, are all read off the same command line, and a function that takes them apart one by
one is a function nobody can add a knob to. They became a `Knobs` value.

**`ARCHITECTURE.md` is unaffected, and was looked at.** Its self-check is about tick steps, what a
step reads or writes, fields on `World` and crates; this adds a method to `World` and none of those.
**`RULES.md` is unaffected**: no rule of the game changed — `set_house_level` is setup, before play,
like `set_terrain`, and the comment on `set_terrain` claiming to be the only such mutation was amended
to name both. **`GLOSSARY.md` is unaffected**: no new word, and *level*, *capacity* and *places* are
all already in it or in `RULES.md`. `ROADMAP.md` gained this row.
